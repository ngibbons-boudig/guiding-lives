## 1. Safeguarding Policy

### Purpose

To protect children and young people from harm and ensure all mentoring practice aligns with UK safeguarding legislation.

### Key Principles

- The welfare of the child is paramount.
- All young people have equal rights to protection.
- Mentors will follow clear reporting procedures for concerns.
- Disclosure handling follows confidentiality boundaries and legal duties.

### Responsibilities

- Maintain an up-to-date Enhanced DBS.
- Follow safeguarding training annually.
- Record and report concerns following the local authority's safeguarding board process.
- Escalate concerns immediately to the designated safeguarding lead (DSL) or referring organisation.

### Procedure for Concerns

- Listen calmly and non-judgmentally.
- Do not promise confidentiality.
- Record facts clearly and promptly.
- Report to DSL/referrer within the same working day.
- Follow statutory guidance if risk of immediate harm.

## 2. Privacy & Data Protection Policy

### Purpose

To explain how client information is collected, stored, used and protected in line with UK GDPR.

### Data Collected

- Referral information
- Session notes
- Contact details
- Risk information

### Lawful Basis

Legitimate interest & safeguarding obligations.

### Data Storage

- Encrypted digital storage only.
- Access restricted to mentor/DSL.
- Retention: records kept for 7 years unless safeguarding concerns require longer.

### Rights of Young People/Parents

- Right to access, request correction, restrict use, or request deletion (unless safeguarding law overrides).

## 3. Confidentiality Policy

### Principles

- Sessions are confidential unless there is a risk of harm to self or others.
- Young people are informed of confidentiality limits at the start.

### Exceptions to Confidentiality

- Safeguarding concerns
- Criminal disclosures (as legally required)
- Court orders

## 4. Complaints Procedure

### Purpose

To ensure concerns are addressed fairly, transparently, and efficiently.

### How to Raise a Complaint

- Submit concern via email or in writing.
- Acknowledgement within 3 working days.
- Full investigation within 10 working days.
- Outcome provided in writing.
- If unresolved, escalate to local authority (if commissioned work) or an external complaints body.

### Record Keeping

All complaints logged and reviewed annually.

## 5. Health & Safety Policy

### Purpose

To ensure safe working practices when mentoring in homes, community venues, or when transporting young people.

### Key Elements

- Risk assessments for all sessions and outings.
- Clear communication with parents/carers and referrers regarding session locations.
- Incident reporting procedures.
- Safe working environment and appropriate equipment.
- Ensure business insurance covers transporting young people.

## 6. Lone Working Policy

### Purpose

To ensure safety during 1:1 mentoring sessions.

### Guidelines

- Share session location, timings, and expected end time with a designated contact.
- When visiting family homes, ensure an adult is present unless agreed otherwise as part of a risk-assessed plan.
- Maintain regular check-ins with your designated safety contact.
- Transporting young people is permitted only with appropriate business insurance, parental consent, and clear risk assessments.

## 7. Risk Assessment Policy

### Purpose

To identify, assess, and manage potential risks to young people, mentors, and others during mentoring activities, including 1:1 sessions, outings, and transportation.

### Scope

This policy applies to all mentoring activities, whether conducted in the community, family homes, public spaces, or during travel in the mentor’s vehicle.

### Principles

- Risk assessments are proportionate and reviewed regularly.
- The aim is to reduce risk while enabling positive, meaningful mentoring experiences.
- Young people’s views and needs are considered as part of the assessment.

### Risk Assessment Process

- Identify potential hazards (environmental, physical, emotional, safeguarding).
- Assess who may be harmed and how.
- Evaluate risk levels and implement control measures.
- Record findings clearly.
- Review and update assessments regularly or following an incident.

### Examples of Risks Considered

- Lone working arrangements
- Travel and transportation in the mentor’s vehicle
- Public or community settings
- Emotional distress or dysregulation
- Medical or additional needs
- Changes in behaviour or risk profile

### Transportation-Specific Controls

- Written parental/carer consent obtained.
- Appropriate business insurance in place.
- Vehicle is roadworthy and maintained.
- Clear boundaries and professional conduct maintained at all times.

### Review

Risk assessments are reviewed at least annually, or sooner if circumstances change.

## 8. Code of Conduct

### Purpose

To set professional expectations for mentors.

### Mentors Will

- Maintain professional boundaries at all times.
- Treat all young people with respect and dignity.
- Uphold confidentiality and safeguarding responsibilities.
- Avoid social media contact with young people.
- Use clear, appropriate communication.
- Ensure all travel and activities are safe, risk assessed, and agreed with parents/carers or referrers.

### Mentors Will Not

- Use discriminatory or inappropriate language.
- Give gifts or accept gifts of high value.
- Share personal information unnecessarily.
